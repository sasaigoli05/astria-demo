Implementation overview · working draft

Nobody leaves without a team, an ID, and someone checking.

Astria is a care navigation platform for people leaving incarceration. It onboards them before release from whatever records exist, resolves coverage, assembles an integrated care team around them, and then keeps checking — at a cadence that relaxes only as the connection proves durable.

01 · the model

Three things, in order

Everything Astria does falls into one of three stages. Each has a different mix of software, AI and human work — and the balance shifts deliberately as you move right.

Mostly automated

1 · Onboard

The facility uploads whatever it has. Astria extracts it, assigns an Astria ID, resolves coverage, and produces a usable starting record — even an incomplete one.

Assisted matching

2 · Assemble

Match to a clinic of record that takes Medicaid and takes this population, plus behavioural health where indicated. Show plainly what the county does not have.

Human, supported

3 · Hold on

An Astria community health worker makes contact and keeps it. Cadence starts weekly and relaxes only as the connection proves real. Silence pulls it tight again.

The one design rule

AI compresses the administrative work so a human can do the relational work more often — never so a human does it less. Every message that reaches a patient is sent by a named person who can be reached back.

04 · onboarding

Give us whatever you have

The design constraint is that fewer than one in twenty state corrections systems can exchange structured medical data. So the intake path is a document pipeline, not an integration — and a partial record is an acceptable, expected output.

1
UploadA reentry coordinator, jail health nurse, or a CHW we place uploads what exists: discharge summary, medication list, programme participation, release date. Scan, fax, PDF, spreadsheet. No format requirement.
2
Extract and normaliseAstria parses it into structured fields — conditions, medications with days of supply, allergies, contacts, release date, programme history. Every field keeps a link back to the page it came from, so a clinician can check the source.
3
Assign the Astria IDA persistent internal identifier that links this person's jail booking number, Medicaid ID and future clinic record numbers. See the next section for what it is and is not.
4
Resolve coverageCheck Medicaid status. If inactive or absent, start the application inside the pre-release window. Coverage active on release day is the single highest-value output of onboarding.
5
Name the gapsThe record is shown with holes visible rather than hidden — missing discharge summary, no contact number, release date unconfirmed. Each gap becomes a chase task with an owner, not a blank field.
6
Hand to a humanA CHW gets a prepared file and a short list of what to confirm. She is not doing data entry; she is doing the part that needs a person.

Two legal routes in

BAA — the county or its health vendor treats Astria as a business associate and discloses for treatment or operations. Fast, scoped to that county.

Patient authorisation — signed pre-release, which makes the record portable after release and is what lets it follow him. Needs careful handling: consent obtained inside carries real questions about voluntariness.

When there is no pre-release window

Jails frequently release with little or no notice. The same pipeline runs post-release: someone walks into a partner organisation on day 4, and onboarding starts there with whatever he is carrying — often a discharge paper and a pill bottle.

Designing for zero notice is not a fallback. For jails it is the primary path.

05 · the astria id

A linking key, not a medical record

What it is

  • A private, commercial, system-level identifier — encrypted at rest and in transit
  • A resolver: the same human across a booking number, a Medicaid ID and three clinic record numbers
  • The thing that lets a record survive reincarceration, a move, or a coverage lapse
  • Queryable only by organisations the person has consented to

What it is not

  • Not a universal personal health record, and not a replacement for an EHR
  • Not a government identifier — a federal appropriations rider has blocked HHS from issuing one since 1999
  • Not visible or useful to corrections, courts, probation or parole. Ever.
  • Not a score, a risk tier, or anything that ranks a person
Say this out loud in every user interview

For someone who has just left custody, a permanent identifier that follows you is the precise shape of the thing they fear. The same object feels like infrastructure or like surveillance depending entirely on who can query it and who controls the consent. If we cannot explain the difference in one sentence to a patient, we have designed it wrong.

06 · assembling the care team

A baseline that exists, and a wishlist that is honest

The team is built in layers. The first layer must be real in every county we operate in. The outer layers are shown as available or not — because pretending a county has a nutritionist does not produce one.

Layer 1 — required

The baseline

  • A clinic of record that takes Medicaid and takes this population
  • Behavioural health or SUD treatment where clinically indicated
  • An Astria community health worker

If layer 1 cannot be assembled, we do not enrol. Saying no is part of the product.

Layer 2 — where it exists

Integrated

  • Pharmacy and medication management
  • Behavioural medicine integrated into primary care
  • Obstetric and reproductive care
  • Care coordinator at the clinic

Billable today in most states. Availability varies sharply by county.

Layer 3 — aspirational

Lifestyle

  • Nutrition and medical nutrition therapy
  • Lifestyle medicine
  • Physical and occupational therapy
  • Group and peer programming

Thin or absent reimbursement today. We build the slot, measure the need, and use it to argue for a value-based arrangement — rather than claim it is covered.

When the match fails

Eighteen North Carolina counties have no health centre. The honest product behaviour, in order: 1. widen the search radius and show the real travel burden; 2. route to a telehealth prescriber for medication continuity where law permits; 3. record the unmet need against that county so the gap becomes evidence rather than a silent failure. We do not enrol someone into a team that does not exist.

07 · continuity

The cadence relaxes. It never ends.

Confidence in someone's continuity is built from signals, not from asking how they are: a prescription filled, a visit attended, coverage still active, a clinic confirming they are on the panel, a message answered. The contact schedule is a function of that confidence.

Week 1–4 · weekly

Fresh out. Everything is fragile and the overdose risk window is open.

Month 2–6 · monthly

Filling prescriptions, attending visits, answering texts.

Month 7–12 · quarterly

Established. We get quiet on purpose.

Any signal breaks

Missed fill, ED admit, bounced text, redetermination approaching — cadence snaps back to weekly. Nobody decides.

Silence is the trigger

Most care systems react to events. The people who fall off stop generating events — that is what falling off means. So the check runs top-down and only absence escalates: pharmacy and claims data first, then a one-click ask to the clinic, then a text to him, then a task for the CHW.

The floor we must set

A tapering cadence needs a minimum contact frequency that no confidence score can drop below. Without it, this is a liability story. What happens to someone the system got quiet about who then dies — how would we know, and whose fault is it? That question needs an answer before launch, not after.

08 · tiers

Four levels Astria operates at

Same platform, different depth. Each tier is a real sale, and each one makes the next easier — a county pilot generates the outcome data a health system wants, which generates the volume a state will contract for.

The CHW is Astria staff at T1 and T2. By T3 and T4 the economics likely push toward their workforce using our tooling — which is a deliberate transition from a services company into a platform company, and it should be planned rather than discovered.

09 · three north carolina counties

What deployment actually looks like

Tier 1

Yadkin County

0 FQHC · 0 DSH · 334 sq mi · nearest health centre 17 mi

One of six NC counties with no health centre and no safety-net hospital. Nothing to match into locally.

  • Astria places one CHW covering Yadkin and a neighbouring county
  • Clinic of record is 17 miles away — transport is a line item, not an afterthought
  • Medication continuity routed to a telehealth prescriber where law allows
  • Success here is simply: a programme exists, and people get to the first visit
Tier 2

Alamance County

6 FQHC · 1 DSH · 423 sq mi · Burlington

Dense safety-net infrastructure and an established FQHC network. The constraint is coordination, not supply.

  • Clinic of record already exists — we handle handoff, coverage and the watch
  • Behavioural health integration is realistic here, so layer 2 is live
  • Billing capture is the commercial argument: work already done, not billed
  • This is where outcome data gets generated for the T3 and T4 sale
Tier 3 / 4

Statewide

100 counties · 288 facilities · 18 with no FQHC

NC has an approved Medicaid reentry demonstration, Healthy Opportunities infrastructure, and NC FIT already operating.

  • The Astria ID becomes the linkage layer across counties and systems
  • Demonstration reporting is produced as a byproduct of the work
  • Partner, don't compete: NC FIT and EMPOWER are the care model, we are the infrastructure
  • Cross-county continuity becomes testable — people move, and today nobody follows

Extremes worth knowing: Dare is furthest from a health centre at 30.5 miles in a straight line across water and sound. Rockingham and Wilkes are large counties — 565 and 754 square miles — with a hospital but no FQHC, which is a different problem from having nothing at all.

11 · what we don't know yet

The list that should make us uncomfortable

Before a pilot

  • Will a county attorney sign a BAA with a startup for this data?
  • Who actually does the upload, and will they keep doing it in month six?
  • Will clinics accept panel attribution for this population — and which ones say no?
  • Does the cadence floor exist, and who is accountable when it fails?

Before scaling

  • What does a pre-release case management encounter actually pay? One state's fee schedule settles it.
  • Does identity resolution hold across booking numbers, Medicaid IDs and MRNs at volume?
  • Can we get pharmacy or claims feeds as a small company, and how long does it take?
  • At what point do CHWs stop being ours — and does the business survive that transition?
Standing rule

Every claim on this page that is not sourced is a hypothesis. The facility data is real and cited. The cadence, the tiers, the implementation timeline and every cost or revenue figure are designed, not validated. Treat them as things to go break.
