Continuity

One identifier that survives the gaps

People who cycle between custody, coverage and community care acquire a new identifier at every door. The Astria ID is the key that resolves them back to the same human being.

Unchanged across every event on the left. Encrypted, internal, and never visible to corrections, courts, probation or parole.

All four identifiers currently resolve to one person and one continuous record.

What it is

  • A private, commercial, system-level identifier, encrypted at rest and in transit
  • A resolver: the same person across a booking number, a Medicaid ID, and clinic records
  • The mechanism that lets a record survive reincarceration, relocation, or a coverage lapse
  • Queryable only by organisations the member has consented to

What it is not

  • Not a personal health record, and not a replacement for an EHR
  • Not a government identifier — federal appropriations language has blocked HHS from issuing one since 1999
  • Not visible or useful to corrections, courts, probation or parole
  • Not a score, a risk tier, or anything that ranks a person
A design constraint we hold to

For someone who has just left custody, a permanent identifier that follows you is the precise shape of the thing they have reason to fear. The same object reads as infrastructure or as surveillance depending entirely on who can query it and who controls consent. If we cannot explain that difference to a member in one sentence, we have designed it wrong.
