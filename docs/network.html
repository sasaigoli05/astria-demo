The network

Astria doesn't build a parallel system

Reentry is never one problem. A person needs primary care and housing and transport and a prescription that doesn't lapse. All of that infrastructure already exists. What's missing is the layer that routes between it.

Where a state already runs a closed-loop referral network — NCCARE360 in North Carolina — Astria routes through it rather than around it. Building a competing referral network would put us in a fight we could not win and should not pick.

Why it's different from a directory

A plan, not a list of phone numbers

Context-aware

A plan reflects what is genuinely near that person — their county, their transport situation, whether they have a phone that works this week.

Tracked to delivery

We distinguish sent from accepted from actually delivered. The gap between the second and third is where most referral systems quietly fail.

Honest about absence

Where a service does not exist locally, Astria records the unmet need against that county instead of issuing a referral nobody can use. The gap becomes evidence.

The open question on this one

Community programmes have no obvious incentive to log into another platform and maintain availability data. Every referral network in the country has struggled with exactly this. Our working answer is that the Care Promoter maintains it as a by-product of the work she is already doing — but it is a real adoption risk, and we would rather name it than pretend it is solved.
