How it works

From ninety days before release to a year after

Three stages. The balance of software and human effort shifts deliberately as you move through them — automated at the front, human at the back.

Mostly automated

1 · Onboard

The facility uploads whatever it has. Argo extracts it, assigns an Astria ID, resolves coverage, and produces a usable record — even an incomplete one.

Assisted matching

2 · Assemble

Match to a clinic that takes Medicaid and takes this population, plus behavioral health where indicated. Where a county has nothing, we record the gap rather than hide it.

Human, supported

3 · Hold on

A Care Promoter makes contact and keeps it. The schedule relaxes as the connection proves durable and tightens the moment a signal breaks.

The journey

One person, step by step

Day −90 to −14

Enrollment begins inside the facility

A reentry coordinator or jail health nurse uploads what exists. Argo extracts conditions, medications and days of supply, release date and contacts. Missing fields become tasks with an owner, not blanks.

Day −90 to −14

Coverage resolved

Medicaid status checked; where coverage is inactive, Argo assembles the application so a certified assister can submit it. The goal is coverage active on release day.

Day −14

A care team is assembled

A clinic of record accepts the referral and puts the person on a panel. Behavioral health or substance-use treatment is added where clinically indicated.

Day 0 · release

A Care Promoter makes contact

In person where we have local staff, by phone or video where we don't. A working phone number and a backup contact are confirmed — the two things most likely to break in month one.

Day 1–30

Weekly contact

First community visit inside thirty days. Medications reconciled and continued before the release supply runs out.

Month 2–6

Monthly, as things hold

Cadence relaxes once prescriptions are filling, visits are happening, and the clinic confirms the person is on its panel.

Month 7–12

Quarterly — and quiet

The goal is an ordinary established patient: named care team, active coverage, seen within the year. Graduation, not permanent membership.

The checking

Silence is the trigger

Most care systems react to events. The people who fall out of care stop generating events — that is what falling out means. So the check runs top-down, and only absence escalates.

Data we already have

Prescription filled, visit attended, coverage still active. Nobody is contacted at all.

Ask the clinic

One click to confirm the person is on the panel and was seen. About five seconds of someone's time.

Text the member

A plain SMS from their Care Promoter. No app, no password, works on a borrowed phone.

A task for a human

If nothing has come back, it becomes a task that week — with everything already assembled.

An open question we take seriously

A tapering schedule needs a floor — a minimum contact frequency no confidence score can drop below. Setting that floor, and naming who is accountable when it fails, is a design decision we are working through with practitioners rather than assuming.
