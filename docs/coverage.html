Proof of concept — North Carolina

We mapped the whole state

A matching engine is only as good as the supply it matches into. So before specifying the product, we mapped every federally qualified health center and safety-net hospital in North Carolina against county geography and population.

North Carolina — care access Health centres per county
15 mi

The finding that shaped the product

No county in North Carolina is more than 31 miles from a county that has a federally qualified health center. We modelled the network: 24 anchor sites would place all 100 counties within a 35-mile radius.

The infrastructure to reach the whole state largely exists. What is missing is the layer that connects it — which is why Astria is a navigation system rather than a clinic.

And the finding that constrains it

At 15 miles — a realistic limit for someone without a car in their first month out — fourteen counties and roughly 734,000 people fall outside reach. Access in North Carolina is not primarily a facility problem. It is a transport problem wearing a facility problem's clothes.

That is why a Care Promoter who can work by phone or video is a design requirement, not a convenience.

How to read these numbers

Distances are straight-line, county-centroid to county-centroid. Treat every one as a floor rather than a drive time — Dare County, on the Outer Banks, is the clearest case where the number flatters reality. We do not yet hold a population or vehicle-access layer, so a large rural county and a dense small one look alike here. Source data is the NC DSH & FQHC Locations 2022 workbook; four county names were misspelled in the source and corrected on load.
